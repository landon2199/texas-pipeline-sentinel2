The plan

Can satellites see pipeline leaks? We compare vegetation along Texas pipelines with similar land nearby, and before and after reported spills, from 2018 to 2026, with Sentinel-2 images at 10 m.

What we are doing

Pipelines are buried, so a leak may first show up as weaker or drier plants along the line. Last year Landon measured greenness (NDVI) and a water index along Texas liquid pipelines for one spring, but with no comparison land and no leak records.

This year we ask two questions. Is vegetation next to pipelines different from similar land 500 to 1,000 m away? And do reported leaks leave a mark: 83 crude oil and fuel spills on Texas pipeline rights-of-way since mid-2018?

We use every Railroad Commission pipeline as published, the EPA ecoregions, soil and land cover data, and Sentinel-2 images for every spring from 2018 to 2026, all in Python in Google Colab.
